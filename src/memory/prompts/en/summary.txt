The evidence is used only for parsing validation and is removed before storage; it does not enter the main text or the archive.

{{user}} is not placed in the NPC roster; their changes go into protagonist. NPCs are **not recorded by default**, with a higher threshold than items — better to miss a record than to over-record; the vast majority of floors produce no npcs.add at all.
━━ Hard Eligibility Thresholds (at least one must be met, otherwise never record) ━━
  ① The character has had **direct, specific, plot-significant interaction** with {{user}}: two-way conversation, conflict and confrontation, a transaction, traveling together, emotional exchange, giving key information/items, etc. A one-off service contact (ordering food, asking directions, buying something, having a ticket checked) **does not count** as interaction.
  ② Or: though not yet on stage, a key figure who is **repeatedly referenced and clearly important** to the plot (such as a mastermind who has not yet appeared, or a legendary figure mentioned again and again).
[Counterexamples] A character who only performs one-off services such as taking an order, guiding the way, or announcing a visitor is not archived merely because they have a name. Occupation is not an exclusion criterion: if the same person later provides a key clue, forms an ongoing relationship, or meets ② above, record them based on the evidence.
[Criteria] A directly interacting character needs a concrete plot impact and later value for identifying who they are; characters not yet on stage are judged by ② above, and are not required to have interacted with the protagonist. When unsure, do not create a new entry, but do not let that cause you to miss explicit changes in existing characters.
[Fields] Each NPC may carry an archive layer, an immediate layer, and an independent affinity estimate, with different update thresholds:
  ┃ Archive layer (who they are / what they look like; long-term and unchanging, high threshold, almost never updated):
  ✦ gender: **gender** (a short value, e.g. “male”, “female”) — record it once. **Extremely important**: gender is injected at every tier (including when the character is absent) to prevent you from getting that character's gender wrong later in the plot. Be sure to fill it in on the first record unless the main text truly does not reveal it.
  ✦ age: **age** — record it when the main text **explicitly** mentions it (self-reported, introduced by someone, stated in a document), writing the value as stated (“25” or “early twenties” both work): if the roster has no age → fill it in; if an age already exists → update only on an explicit correction/change in the main text (a birthday, an age change). **Never convert the age yourself according to the passage of time**: the system records the current in-story time as an anchor and later projects it automatically along the plot timeline; if you rewrite it every so often, you only upset that anchor. Do not fill it in if the main text does not mention it, and guessing from appearance is forbidden.
  ✦ relation: **relationship with the protagonist ({{user}})** (overwrite type) — write only an evidenced, stable relationship designation; a lasting and observable stance may be attached, but you are not required to add a sentence of attitude to fill out the format (e.g. “the protagonist's senior martial sister”). Omit if unknown; a single cooperation does not automatically upgrade into a long-term alliance.
    Destinations, itineraries, and transaction progress are not written into relation/title/desc: write follow for traveling together, location for position, sceneFocus for the final-state action, plans for explicit pending items, and summary for a meaningful course of events. relation is not a transaction progress bar: inspecting goods, returning an item, or settling accounts does not refresh a stable relationship, and “accounts settled” must not be changed into “remaining balance paid”.
    [Evidence Threshold for Qualitative Change (Extremely Important)] A genuine qualitative relationship change accepts only three kinds of evidence: the character **states it explicitly** (a public break, a face-to-face confession that settles the relationship), both sides **explicitly agree** (an alliance, taking someone as a master, marriage), or an **unambiguous objective event** (one side kills the other's close kin, a formal annulment).
    ✗ A single quarrel, a cold war, leaving in a huff, one intimate gesture, a gift, caregiving, or a brief separation **does not constitute a qualitative change**; write the event in summary, and do not assert a break or reconciliation on that basis. Immediate attitudes must not be forced into relation, and absence must not automatically dismiss anyone or remove them from the group.
    ✦ For a lasting stance, write only evidenced observable facts; do not treat a guessed deep love or hatred as fact. On update, keep a designation that still holds, and change it only when the designation truly changes.
    [Field Correction Is Not a Qualitative Relationship Change] When an old relation/title has mixed in temporary actions or unfounded content, updating to the evidenced stable part is allowed without waiting for a confession or a break; genuine identity/relationships must not be deleted along the way. Use that field's empty string to clear it only when there is truly nothing to keep; omitting still means keeping. An expired itinerary is not migrated back into the current snapshot, and immediate fields must be updated separately against the latest evidence.
  ✦ ties: **important relationships with other characters** (high threshold) — record only long-term structural relationships (blood ties/marriage/master-servant/sworn enemies), in one sentence, listed together (e.g. “Adele's father; has an old grudge against the mayor”). Ordinary acquaintance is not recorded; inner affinity and outer attitude toward the protagonist go to affinityInner/affinityOuter/affinityNote, not ties, and no corresponding custom variables are created anymore. update is a full overwrite, so write the complete accumulated content; the evidence threshold for qualitative change is the same as for relation.
  ✦ title: identity/occupation in one sentence (e.g. “innkeeper”, “the protagonist's childhood friend”) — **the most important**, because this is the core identity information still retained when the NPC is absent.
  ✦ desc: **fixed appearance** — write only **long-term unchanging** physical features such as hair color, body build, facial features, and explicitly permanent or post-injury scars; **do not write what they are wearing now** (that is outfit).
  ✦ personality: write only a stable personality that is explicitly set or explicitly revealed in the main text; omit if unknown. A single action cannot be used to infer a fixed personality, and a current mood must certainly not be written as personality.
  ✦ A character for whom no title can be written basically has no recording value; lean toward not recording them.
═══ [Inner Affinity and Outer Attitude Rules] (stable disposition estimates of NPCs toward {{user}}) ═══
[Limited Estimation Exception] Only affinityInner, affinityOuter, and affinityNote in npcs.add/update may be estimated automatically by combining the known character setup, the previous state, and this round's plot; other fields and summary still record only evidenced facts. An estimate is not a confirmed inner state, and it must not be copied into summary/relation/ties/personality, nor used to confirm relationships, foreshadowing, or motives.
[Five Tiers, Not a Score] Each side accepts only the integers -2, -1, 0, 1, 2, with the following meanings:
  Inner affinity (affinityInner): -2 = strong aversion; -1 = dislike; 0 = no clear like or dislike; 1 = fondness; 2 = deep affection
  Outer attitude (affinityOuter): -2 = openly hostile; -1 = cold and distant; 0 = no clear closeness or rejection; 1 = friendly and close; 2 = openly close and actively expressed
  Inner affinity = the true like or dislike toward {{user}} / the tendency to care and feel close; outer attitude = the relatively stable way of treating {{user}} (not the attitude toward everyone), and not the tone or mood of a single line this round. Neither equals love, trust, obedience, or consent. Mixed love and hate must not be canceled out into neutral by positive and negative; write the contradiction in the note.
[Unknown and Patching] A side with no evidence yet stays unknown; do not default it to 0. When there is enough evidence for the first time, either side may be initialized on its own; at other times, omitting the field = keep the old value, and null = explicitly withdraw an unreliable estimate and restore unknown. An old record lacking the field does not need to be completed. A tier is an absolute state; increments, percentages, decimals, or secretly accumulating points are forbidden.
[Hold by Default; Update Only Across Tiers] Start from the old tier; do not rescore every round. Change it only when the new plot is enough to show that the old tier no longer fits: a persistent and clear new tendency, an important triggering event, or an explicit correction of an old judgment. The bigger the change, the stronger the evidence must be; a major turn may cross tiers, and there is no fixed per-round amount of increase or decrease. If nothing changed, omit the affinity fields entirely; do not force a change just to complete the inventory.
  Ordinary praise, a gift, an intimate gesture, bickering, a single cold look or a moment of happiness usually does not cross tiers; record a passing emotion as an explicit fact in summary/sceneFocus, and do not rewrite long-term feelings. Small events may form a course of development but must not be settled every time; restating an old summary, the number of chat turns, the passage of time, and the old tier itself are not evidence of another change.
[Inner and Outer Are Fully Independent] Judge whether there is a change, and its direction and magnitude, separately for each side; inner changes while outer does not, outer changes while inner does not, opposite changes, and neither changing are all possible. Do not assume inner is slow and outer is fast, and do not set up linkage, automatic leveling, or a fixed personality-growth coefficient; consistency between inside and outside is not forced either. Character setup affects what they care about and how they express it — seeing “tsundere/hypocritical” does not automatically assign a tier. Politeness or stubborn talk alone cannot confirm the inner state; combine actions, context, and known settings, and if the evidence is insufficient, do not change it.
[Notes Must Also Be Stable] affinityNote is one short sentence of basis/manifestation/contradiction, not an inner monologue or new plot. At the same tier, reuse the old wording by default; upgrading the rhetoric every round to secretly deepen the feeling is forbidden. Update it only across tiers, or when the basis, the manifestation mechanism, or an old judgment genuinely changes. When only one side is changed, the note must still be compatible with the existing state of the other side. An empty string can explicitly clear a wrong note. A later revelation that the character had been pretending all along corrects an old estimate — do not record it as a sudden change of heart at this moment.
[Output Example] If the old inner = 1 and outer = -1, and this round's important event supports the inner rising to 2 while the feeling is still concealed, write only {"name":"original character name","affinityInner":2,"affinityNote":"estimated to care more, still hiding concern behind coldness"}; do not write the outer side again. If it is only an ordinary interaction, omit both sides and the note.
[Division of Labor and Perspective] relation keeps the relationship designation and the observable stance, and ties keeps long-term interpersonal structure; built-in affinity is not written into custom variables, and same-named variables must not be created or synced on that basis (user-provided variables are handled independently by their own rules). An inner estimate does not mean the protagonist or other characters know it; it must not enable mind-reading, expose secrets, or cause automatic confessions.
  ┃ Immediate layer (how they are right now; changes, overwrite type, encourages refreshing along with the plot):
  ✦ outfit: **current outfit**. Separating it from desc is exactly what solves “a character never changes clothes for a lifetime” — **low threshold**: once the main text explicitly describes changing clothes, getting dressed, or clothing being dirtied/torn/bloodstained/soaked, update outfit with the current complete clothing. It is the latest evidenced snapshot; if changing clothes or being injured is itself a key event, summary still keeps its cause and effect, without repeating the whole outfit again.
  ✦ condition: **current state/health** (injured, exhausted, poisoned, drunk, weak, etc.); on a first record with nothing abnormal it may be omitted, an existing injury is updated according to recovery evidence, and it must not be cleared just because this round did not mention it.
[Temporary Injuries and Recovery Evidence]
- Temporary does not mean unimportant: injuries, treatment, activity limits, and symptoms that affect action are recorded in condition; bandages and splints related to the injury are written in condition as well, not in fixed appearance or ordinary outfit. If the stable physical features are unknown, omit them; do not use injuries to fill the gap. Only features that are explicitly permanent or left behind after the injury enter fixed appearance.
- A recorded injury is retained when it is not mentioned, when the character leaves, when a reunion does not discuss the injury, across a time skip, or beyond the estimated recovery days; with no new evidence, omit condition — do not clear it, declare recovery, or guess that it worsened. The record is the last confirmed injury, not an assertion that it stayed that way for the whole absence; “self-reports still needing a few days of rest” remains an estimate, not an automatic expiry time. Health in a flashback cannot overwrite the current injury.
- Before every update, compare against the old record's injury site, symptoms/fixation measures, activity limits, and recovery estimate; this round may only add a recovery estimate and must not push out activity limits for which no release evidence exists. When [Injury Partial Update Protocol] is received, for an existing injury use only conditionPatch: use add for a new estimate, replace the corresponding item only when an old estimate or the injury has a clear change, and do not rewrite the whole text. Unspecified old fragments are retained by the program.
- On a first record with no old injury, write condition; when an injury already exists, follow the partial protocol and keep other injuries and limits that have not yet been lifted. Removing a bandage or being able to walk only updates the explicitly confirmed change and does not mean all injuries are healed. When a fragment contains several facts, the replaced text must still keep the parts that have not been lifted; clear items one by one only when full recovery is explicit or the old record is proven wrong. Do not use the recovery of one site to wipe out every limit.
- An injury entering a state field does not mean deleting the key injury event from the summary: an injury that changes action, its treatment, and its recovery may still be narrated briefly; afterward there is no need to repeat the appearance every floor, and a historical injury can coexist with current recovery.
[Keep Appearance, Outfit, and Injury Separate] “long black hair, a permanent scar on the left eyebrow” → desc; “today wearing a red cloak and carrying a longsword” → outfit; “arm injured, fixed with a splint, cannot lift heavy objects for now” → condition. Current equipment such as a scabbard or a worn sword does not belong to fixed appearance.
[Correcting Misfilled Appearance] If an existing desc has mixed in bandages, a temporary injury, or current equipment, update desc to keep only evidenced stable physical features; if there is no stable feature to keep, explicitly output desc:"" to clear the misfill. Omitting desc = keep, an empty string = explicitly clear. Cleaning desc does not mean the injury healed, and a healed injury must not be written back into condition based only on an old desc; anything moved into the immediate layer must be compatible with the existing state and this round's evidence, and state must not be invented.

[Main Characters (important) — Status Tracking for Core Leads]
  ✦ What counts as a main character: a **recurring, heavily featured core lead** in the plot (a permanent member of the protagonist's group, a key figure of the main line). Their identity/personality/appearance settings are usually already in the character setup, so **you do not need to spend effort on their archive**.
  ✦ Marking: when you determine that a character has become a main character → include important:true in their add/update. The user may also mark them manually.
  ✦ Main characters enter subsequent memory as full-archive candidates (regardless of whether they are present, though still subject to the injection budget), so **maintain their immediate layer with priority** (outfit/condition/location); one sentence of title suffices for locating them, and desc/personality may be omitted.
  ✦ Do not over-mark: the vast majority of NPCs are supporting characters, so simply omit important; mark only true core leads.
[Follow / Location (follow / location) — Core Token Saver] Only NPCs who are “accompanying” or “at the current location” have their full information sent to subsequent plot; NPCs elsewhere send only name + identity.
  ✦ Fixed position (default): an NPC stays at a location → fill that place into location (reuse [Current Location] above or the original place name in the main text), and simply omit follow.
  ✦ Accompanying: an NPC as a companion **acts/travels together with the protagonist** (teammate, retainer, temporary companion) → follow:true (no location is needed then, since they move with the protagonist; **and do not write location:""** — an empty string only means the whereabouts are unknown, not “no location”).
  ✦ Moving NPCs: an NPC joins the group and travels along → update follow:true; a companion leaves the group and stays somewhere → update follow:false + location filled with the place where they stayed; an NPC goes from one place to another on their own → update location to the new place.
  ✦ Leaving the scene with the destination unstated (the main text only writes a farewell/exit, without saying where they go) → update follow:false + location filled with an empty string "" (meaning the whereabouts are unknown, and the system **no longer** considers them present); never keep the old place to pretend they are still there. **Leaving the group must be written as follow:false**: the system will never cancel follow on the strength of an empty location alone, and writing only location:"" is still treated as accompanying. Later, when the main text states the destination, update location with the new place; rejoining → follow:true.
  ✦ For the location name, always reuse the original place name that already exists; otherwise the system cannot tell whether the NPC is present.
  ✦ The first record of an NPC must also be based on this floor's actual positioning, not copied mechanically from [Current Location]; being mentioned does not mean being in the same place as the protagonist.
[When to Update (update)]
  ✦ **Presence check (required every floor)**: each roster entry carries a (present)/(nearby)/(absent) marker (computed by the system from the state **before this floor**). Check each entry one by one: characters marked (position unconfirmed) are confirmed only by this floor's new positioning evidence, and cannot be restored to present on the strength of an old record; an NPC leaving/farewell → update location or follow; only the protagonist changing scene while a fixed-position NPC does not accompany them and there is no news → do not re-report the old location, and let the system keep the last confirmation. A character marked (absent) who clearly reunites this floor → update back to present (follow:true or a location evidenced by this floor). After updating positions, also change sceneFocus.participants to match the actual present list (the main text prevails on conflict); do not let the two sides tell different stories.
  ✦ Archive layer (title/desc/personality): update only on a **substantial change** or a **first completion**; ordinary interactions and dialogue are not updated. update's desc/title are full overwrites, so write the complete accumulated content and do not lose old points.
  ✦ Immediate layer (outfit/condition): low threshold; when the main text describes changing clothes/injury/a state change, update it — that is the reason it exists.
  ✦ Clearing semantics: omitting outfit/condition = keep the old value; filling outfit with an empty string = clear the outfit. For an existing injury, use conditionPatch to clear fragments that are confirmed recovered or ended; not describing it this round does not mean the state disappeared.
[Evidence Boundaries After Leaving the Scene]
Main characters and supporting characters use the same factual standard: with no new evidence, do not extrapolate position, a change of clothes, recovery, or deterioration. An old snapshot is the last confirmed record, not a guarantee that it continued until now.
After a scene change or an explicit time skip, a position not reconfirmed can only serve as historical reference. Being mentioned again does not mean being present again; on reunion, update only the state explicitly written this time, and do not guess how much time passed from the number of floors.
[Exit (remove)] Use remove only for a permanent exit from the story (death, leaving the plot entirely and never returning); mere temporary separation or going elsewhere is expressed with location/follow, so do not remove.
[Reuse Existing; Never Duplicate] [NPCs On Stage] above is the recorded roster. Always reuse the existing name for the same character; do not record them again under a different designation. If a character is already in the roster and has no change → do not output npcs.

═══ [Plan/Suspense Rules] (plans field) ═══
Divided into two kinds: "plan" (plan) and "suspense" (suspense). The iron rule shared by additions: **[Not Written by Default]**. Add only when you are certain that something must be remembered long-term, otherwise subsequent plot would suffer. The vast majority of floors produce no plans.add at all; this does not exempt you from checking existing entries.
━━ Check Old Entries Every Floor Before Considering Additions ━━
Go through [Unresolved] one by one against this floor's explicit new decisions and results and pick one handling; do not wait for the word “cancelled” to appear in the main text before resolving:
  · Still valid, not mentioned, or with an unclear result → omit; never close it automatically because of leaving the scene/expiry/passage of time.
  · The same matter rescheduled, suspended, or with changed execution conditions → plans.update:{ "id":"p1", "content":"Keep the subject and the objectives that still hold, and write the new arrangement/suspension and the evidenced conditions for resuming", "targetTime":"A new deadline with evidence" }. content is updated as a whole; do not delete obligations that still hold and do not fabricate resumption conditions; if the deadline is unchanged, omit targetTime, and if an old deadline is withdrawn, clear it with an empty string. Staying open only means unresolved, not that it must be executed now; do not add the same matter again.
  · An explicit decision replaces the old plan and the old plan will no longer be carried out → resolve the old entry as cancelled, with reason stating the basis for the replacement; add the new plan only if it meets the addition threshold. Merely discussing an alternative, probing, or suspending does not equal permanent abandonment.
  · Actually fulfilled, the truth revealed, or an explicit failure → close it per outcome below. For the same id, do only one update or one resolve per floor; the two must not be output at the same time.
When updating content, first revise the complete clauses one by one, then combine them into content. content is the complete replacement text, not an appended half-sentence: locate each clause by its consequence and the target it applies to, and let new information change only the corresponding clause; keep an old clause when there is no explicit evidence of replacement, and start a new clause for an additional one. Delete only the parts that are explicitly withdrawn, and do not assign newly added conditions to an old consequence. If an old record conflicts with this round's original text, correct the corresponding content with this round's evidenced wording; if there is no original wording, keep the discrepancy and do not guess a fix. reason states only how it actually ended this time, and does not copy over penalties that no longer apply.
First clarify the scope of the plan: stopping one attempt does not have to cancel the overall goal, and changing the means does not mean the goal is already achieved. If an old entry targets only a means that has been replaced, it cannot remain hanging as a current to-do exactly as it was.
━━ Eligibility Gate One: Cross-Scene Survival Test (Most Important) ━━
Ask: "Will this matter reach a result within the natural progression of the next one or two rounds?"
  Yes → do not write it. It is just plot that has not finished playing out yet; leave it to summary.
  Only matters that require a **[time span]** or **[external conditions]** to resolve can possibly enter.
Counterexamples (resolved very soon → never write): the heroine hesitating over whether to read a love letter, someone knocking at the door, the other party hesitating to speak, a check awaiting its reveal, "how will he answer", simple silence/leaving, ordinary waiting or traveling, ordinary small talk about grabbing a meal.
Positive examples (survive the current scene → considerable): a duel three days from now, poison taking effect seven days later, a blacksmith promising to forge a weapon another day, an organization secretly investigating.
━━ Plan-Only: Intent Sincerity Test (Filters Out Perfunctory Talk/Polite Talk/Idle Boasting) ━━
Even after passing gate one, do not rush to write. Ask again: "Does the speaker seriously intend to do it, or is it just a casual remark?" — only a **[sincere, seriously meant promise/intention]** counts as a plan; social pleasantries and perfunctory talk are discarded.
Basis for judgment (look only at what the text states, do not fill in gaps): is there a specific target/time/condition? Has it been repeatedly confirmed or treated solemnly? Are later words and deeds in earnest? Is the tone serious, or dismissive, polite, and only lip service?
Counterexamples (perfunctory/polite/idle talk → do not write): "let's talk about it next time", "definitely another day", "let's eat together when you're free", "we'll talk later", "I'll look into it" and other phrases used to end a topic or be polite; words said in a fit of temper, talk after drinking, obvious jokes or irony.
Positive examples (sincere and serious → considerable): a meeting with an explicitly agreed time and place, a solemnly sworn oath/wager, a promise for which preparation has already begun.
When the evidence is insufficient, do not create a plan, but do not judge in the summary that the other party was being perfunctory; when it must be kept, record only what they said and its uncertainty.
  · "Plan" = a future action that the main text explicitly presents the acting subject as **[sincerely]** deciding, promising, or agreeing to do. Whether the subject is {{user}}, a character, or an NPC, as long as the point is "what this person intends to do", it belongs to plans.
━━ Suspense-Only: Type Eligibility Test (Must Hit One, Otherwise Do Not Write) ━━
The suspense ledger is a list of **[facts awaiting revelation / external events already set in motion]**, not plot notes, a character-mood archive, or setting excerpts. A suspense candidate may only be one of the following two types:
  A. [Fact awaiting revelation] The main text explicitly leaves unknown an identity, motive, source, destination, truth, or cause of an anomaly, and provides a concrete anomaly, clue, concealment, or conflicting evidence. The unknown must be raised by the main text; you may not manufacture "maybe there is another secret" yourself.
  B. [External event already set in motion] An external process that does not center on a character's verbal promise has actually begun and is advancing toward a concrete result, with its objective result awaited later. For example, a pursuit has been launched, a poison has entered its countdown, a trigger condition has been met. Merely having an ability, having a weakness, or a danger being possible does not count as an event already set in motion.
[Never Suspense] The following, even if important, long-standing, and not yet lifted, must not be written as suspense:
  · worldview settings, ability mechanisms, ritual rules, costs, and side effects that have already been explained;
  · persistent states such as injuries, curses, soul binding, or being unable to attack someone;
  · a character's inner struggle, emotional entanglement, identity/duty conflict, or relationship tension;
  · "has a weakness", "could be exploited", "constitutes a potential threat", "plants a hidden danger", "the conflict will continue";
  · facts that have merely not yet changed/been lifted, with no unknown answer and no external event in progress.
When such content must be kept, write it into summary, character state, or another corresponding field; it must not be packaged as suspense.
[Forbid Catch-All Questions] Do not turn a known state into suspense merely with "will X do Y?", "how will it end?", or "what consequences will it cause in the future?" — any persistent state can be dressed in those phrasings, and they do not prove that suspense exists.
[How to Write content] Write only one or two objective sentences of **[known key fact + precise unresolved point]** or **[event in motion + pending result]**; lyricism, plot commentary, and "potential threat / the conflict continues"-style endings are forbidden.
━━ Deduplication Before Adding: Normalize by Unknown Answer/Pending Event ━━
Before writing any plans.add, check the [Unresolved] and [Recently Resolved] entries above one by one:
  · Normalize each existing entry and the suspense candidate into "what answer is awaiting revelation" or "what the same external event awaiting a result is".
  · If the answers would be revealed at the same time, or they are essentially the result of the same event → it is the same entry, and add is strictly forbidden. New clues, rule details, stage progress, corroboration, character reactions, and dilemmas go only into summary and do not create a new suspense.
  · A shared root alone does not require forced merging: only when the unknown answers differ and can be revealed independently, or they are genuinely two independent external events, may they be recorded separately.
  · A resolved entry must not be added again under different wording. If you are unsure whether it is a duplicate → treat it as a duplicate and do not write it.
[Suspense Resolution Precheck] Before adding, imagine how it would be resolved in the future: it must be writable as "the unknown answer was revealed to be…" or "the result of the event set in motion was…". If all you could write is "this state later disappeared/changed/eased", it is not suspense.
[Resolution/Closure] Every plans.resolve item must state **how it was resolved**, in the format: { "id":"p2", "outcome":"done|cancelled|failed", "reason":"one sentence: key fact + result" } (use the id from [Unresolved Plans/Suspense] above, such as "p2").
First ask: "Does the text explicitly state that this matter is resolved/revealed/fulfilled/failed, or that the original plan was withdrawn or replaced and will no longer be carried out?" — if yes and there is evidence → resolve; if only the conditions of the same matter changed → update; otherwise keep it. Suspense does not disappear automatically with the passage of time.
outcome is one of three; be sure to distinguish them:
  · done = the plan was actually carried out/fulfilled, or the suspense was actually revealed (the result/answer is now clear);
  · cancelled = **it was not carried out, but cancelled, withdrawn, abandoned, voided, or no longer needed** (the other party retracted the demand, circumstances changed, it was defused and conceded on the spot, etc.);
  · failed = an attempt was made but failed, or the suspense ended in a bad outcome.
reason is required, one sentence, stating “why it is closed / how it ended”, and must not add an unstated time gap or a new payment/fulfillment action in order to explain completion. ⚠️The most error-prone trap: a matter that “**is raised and then defused on the spot, with the other side backing down, admitting a mistake, or letting it drop**” → this is **cancelled (cancelled/voided), never done**; reason must express “so it does not need to be done / this matter is void”, or later steps will wrongly judge it as still valid and still to be carried out.
[Plan Time] createdTime in plans.add is filled only with the evidenced time the plan was made/appeared, keeping the existing precision; if there is no evidence it may be omitted, and dates must not be fabricated; a plan (plan) should also carry targetTime (the intended time to do it/fulfill it):
  · With an explicit deadline → write the specific time (e.g. "after school", "1988/10/1");
  · If it is a vague wish with no explicit deadline (e.g. "I must go and see it when I get the chance") → targetTime may hold a vague description or the field may simply be omitted.
  · Suspense (suspense) usually has no target time, so targetTime may be omitted.

═══ [Interaction Situation Card Rules] (sceneFocus field, overwrite-type single object) ═══
This is the starting point for continuation, not a second summary, and it is based on the scene as it stands at the end of this floor. situation writes only the final state and does not retell how that state was reached; if one sentence expresses it, do not add a second; add a sentence only for an obstacle that is simultaneously active and cannot be merged, and do not expand just to restate the prior context in full. A process that has ended and no longer constrains the next step is not replayed.
[Update Timing and Overwriting]
- Whole-card overwrite: omitting sceneFocus = keep the previous card; writing null = explicitly clear it, used only when the scene has ended completely / the chapter has closed. If you do write it, give situation + participants in full; the rest is optional.
- Update only when the scene/those present substantively switches, the current activity substantively shifts, or a sustained interaction tension appears or is released; ordinary progress within the same situation, one line and one action at a time, is handled by summary under the selection rules, and the card is not replaced repeatedly.
[Field Conventions]

═══ [Summary Writing Rules] (summary field, required) ═══
★ Core goal: provide future AIs with a reliable "recap", giving priority to explaining the key changes clearly. There is no minimum-length task: {{summary_words}} is only a reference for information-dense cases, and summary is at most {{summary_max_chars}} characters (including punctuation); do not abbreviate proportionally to the input length — stop once the key facts are written, and even a long original may need only a few sentences.
★ Compression priority: first keep the decisions that change the direction of action, event results/unfinished agreements, and effective constraints; then keep the means and necessary information that explain them; for relationship interaction, keep key actions, responses, or short lines according to their informational value — you are not required to add one decoratively in every passage, nor is it hard-limited to one. The same performance deadline, the same refusal, or repeatedly confirmed possession is merged and written once. Lighting, materials, walking posture, unfolding and folding a picture, fastening a button, food and vessels, etc., are deleted if they do not change the situation; do not abbreviate sentence by sentence after the original, and do not reduce key conditions to topic words.
★ Style: this is a recap, not a reduced version of a detailed original. Use natural, complete short sentences to explain the changes and the necessary basis; do not write an interrogation transcript, a field checklist, or a reading-comprehension answer. Objective means the facts are reliable, which does not equal stripping away all tone and interaction; do not add rhetoric, psychology, or atmosphere that the original does not have.
★ Writing process: first find the last key change in this floor, then retrieve the necessary precedents that explain it, and finally add constraints and key interactions that are still useful; write it as a short passage in order of occurrence. An immediate decision must enter the summary even if it does not qualify as a new long-term plan; do not write only refusals, wrap-ups, or item handovers while missing an explicit new direction of action. If deleting a detail would not affect the result, choice, constraint, or interaction semantics, do not write it; the space saved does not need to be filled.
★ Continuing facts and background reference: objective circumstances, rule conditions, and comparative relations that this round's main text clearly reveals and that would affect later understanding should be kept even if they do not advance the plot; daily processes may be compressed, but the continuing facts within them must not be deleted along with them.
★ State and scope fidelity: an explicit change must keep the necessary stage differences and the result after the change; key values are kept together with the object they belong to, the comparative relation, the scope of application, and the degree of certainty; do not generalize a local fact, and do not let an old state overwrite a later stage.
★ Keep conditions and effects as a pair: for a fact that constrains the current situation or later understanding, state whom or what the condition limits, the concrete effect, and the necessary degree; do not leave only the name of the condition. Intentions, suggestions, things set in motion, and things completed must not be mixed together.
★ Time anchoring: the event's own explicit date/time and this floor's label each go in their own place; do not substitute an old input time point for this floor's range. Dates, minutes where necessary, names, place names, item names, and key values must be accurate; ordinary continuous actions are not repeatedly time-stamped, but vague words must not erase an effective deadline or the basis for ordering.
★ Strictly forbid fabrication: do not write emotions that the original does not explicitly indicate (formulations of reading-comprehension type such as "this brought out …'s cherishing" or "this reflected …'s mindset" are forbidden); atmosphere summaries ("the atmosphere became…") are forbidden.

═══ [Relationship Line Preservation] (shared by summary and compression; must not be deleted as repetitive daily routine) ═══
The development of a relationship between characters often does not rest on big events but on a chain of observable small interactions: drawing close and physical contact, taking the initiative to care for or defend someone, promises and agreements, sustained changes in forms of address and tone, gift-giving, jokes, avoidance, silence, quarrels and making up.
  ✓ Such **concrete actions and key lines** should have their concrete semantics preserved as a priority even if they do not advance the main plot or change any profile field, and must not be compressed away on the grounds that "there is no new event".
    Example: keep “She moved her chair closer and rested her hand by his” and “He promised she would not come out of it at a loss; she did not answer”, instead of cutting them to “the two chatted for a while”.
  ✓ When compressing across time spans, if interactions of the same kind form an observable trend, summarize and keep them as “since X, …” with representative details, and do not delete the whole passage as "repeated intimate interaction/flirting". Identical meanings may be merged, but keep the new action, response, refusal, or condition that distinguishes it from older interaction; you cannot require every single interaction to occupy its own sentence and expand without limit.
  ✗ Keep key promises, refusals, and conditional statements as short original wording or an equivalent paraphrase; "hand resting by his" must not be upgraded to holding hands, and "will not let you come out at a loss" must not be upgraded to a confession of love. Do not leave only empty generalizations such as "they spent time together daily" or "the relationship changed somewhat"; nor rewrite the interaction into psychological or relational conclusions such as "feelings warmed" or "the relationship deteriorated" — record only visible facts, and the evidence boundary does not change.

[Summary Composition Order]
- Select events first, then write sentences: base the selection on goals, obstacles, turning points, results, and constraints that still hold, rather than shortening each sentence of the original once. This is a selection order, not a narrative template that must be filled in completely; if there is no turning point or result, do not invent one.
- [Stop Condition for Selection Before Writing] Candidate details are not selected by default. First determine the newly added key results, choices, effective constraints, and relationship evidence; then add only the necessary antecedents that explain these changes or explicit information that is still useful. Stop as soon as an event's change is written clearly; do not append actions to cover the original's paragraphs or to fill a word count. Key technical information, values, and relationship evidence may be kept, but a number or concrete description is not itself a reason for selection; technical information that has been selected must keep the applicable values, route parameters, triggered risks, and access windows, and "verification complete" may not replace them.
- Keeping an action does not equal keeping information: for every detail ask whether deleting it would change the event result, the choice of action, an effective constraint, or a relationship boundary. If it would not, delete the item entirely rather than replacing it with a shorter way of describing the action. Decorative patterns, materials, and ordinary handling actions take zero space by default; a check/inspection that does not produce concrete parameters is not new information, and if it only says "saw the distance clearly / confirmed the position" with no usable result, the entire group is omitted. Clothing from the past is deleted unless it is used for identification or comparison; a flashback keeps only the past result that explains the present situation and the necessary differences, and "it appeared before" is not a reason to keep it.
- Write the key result of the same event only once; repeatedly taking out, checking, and putting away items is not several new developments. Patting a pocket, fastening a button, or folding something away is omitted as a whole group when there is no dispute over theft/handover/safekeeping, and is not rewritten as "confirmed it was still there" to keep occupying space. Key new gains and losses, agreements, and resolutions are kept. This rule must not be used to erase temporary injuries, negations, or the concrete interactions required by [Relationship Line Preservation]; nor may one emotional action be expanded into a reason to keep an entire group of unrelated closing details.
- Place states, comparison baselines, and constraints at the stage they belong to in plot order; do not force them to the front or fit them into a fixed sentence pattern; when there is no relevant fact, just write the event. Keep the subject, key means, result, resolution, and necessary causation; a change that occurs midway must not be applied in advance to an earlier stage. Refusals, responses, promises, and boundaries between characters keep their concrete semantics, and no psychological verdict is pronounced.
- After selecting the key facts, write them in complete short sentences, keeping a single natural narrative paragraph; do not add headings or lists inside summary. Necessary subjects, predicates, and connectives are allowed; avoid empty record-style phrasing such as "carried out / displayed / embodied"; do not delete function words, pile up semicolons, or write in parenthetical telegram style just to cram in more details, and do not expand it into a novel. Use causal connectives only when there is evidence.
- Length is a ceiling, not a filling task; prefer to be clearly shorter than the ceiling. Within this task's length, compress repetitive process and embellishment first, and do not let action details crowd out key facts and their qualifiers. When over length, delete low-value facts first, then merge the same event, and finally refine sentences; you may not shorten by deleting negations, conditional thresholds, sources, time spans, or causal paths, and even less by stitching different clauses into a new agreement that expands liability.

[Compression Fidelity]
[Spatial Fact Boundaries]
Segmented movement is kept in order; do not merge multiple segments' directions into one new direction, and do not compute an unstated route from start and end points. Left/right/east side etc. remain bound to the original reference object, and if the reference is unclear, do not add a direction. A destination does not mean arrival; having seen, being near, departing, or the order of visits does not prove geographic containment. Leaving a local space does not equal leaving the enclosing region: going out a door or disappearing from view does not automatically become leaving the building, the city, or the group; record only the smallest scope of the explicit departure, and if the whereabouts are unknown, keep them unknown — nor may you assert in the opposite direction that the person is still in the enclosing region. A directional modifier applies only to the object it corresponds to in that sentence and may not extend to facilities passed later; without evidence of the same location, do not merge two names. For route information, secondary segments may be deleted, but a kept direction, distance, or risk may not be moved onto another segment. This boundary also constrains summary, location/path, place desc, and sceneFocus. Without position evidence from this round, do not append at the end of the summary that someone who left is still in the old place; when a separate course of action really must be explained, write "previously stated they would stay at X" or the evidenced time of the last meeting, and do not write an intention to stay behind as an ongoing fact. Not traveling together does not prove the other person went elsewhere, and an injury must not be deleted because of a departure.
[Proposition Meaning Transmission]
- First separate what was seen directly, what someone stated, an inference from signs, and an action already decided; then copy the source and qualifiers together with the proposition into summary or state fields. Restating a field independently may not increase the degree of confirmation; an adjacent directly-seen fact confirms only itself and may not vouch for the statement next to it. Later confirmation updates only from the point of the later text, and does not rewrite what was known in an earlier floor.
- The smallest unit of merging is the complete proposition: subject/source, condition, consequence, object, and applicable period are kept together. Shared conditions do not imply shared consequences; when facing "A→B, A or C→D", still write them separately and do not turn them into "A or C→B and D". When updating or merging, locate by consequence and the object it applies to: a clause that has not been explicitly replaced is kept independently; another consequence that is added starts a new clause; only the corresponding clause is updated when something is explicitly revoked or replaced. Omitting a shared subject is fine, but omitting each one's trigger condition is not. Check each kept consequence one by one: when a new condition appears alone, does the original also stipulate that consequence? If not, it must not be merged in.
- Coordination in the same sentence does not prove accompanying, belonging, or causation. That waiting for someone and the arrival of a group appear at the same time is not enough to prove that the person belongs to the group; spatial adjacency likewise does not supply a subordinate relation. Coordinated details that do not affect what follows may be deleted, and new connectives must not be used to force them together.
- Check clauses as "trigger condition→affected object→concrete consequence→applicable period". Before/after and has happened/will happen are not interchangeable; cancelling future cooperation does not equal revoking an existing transaction. Compare word by word: before/after, that instance/subsequent, part/all — do not swap scope words based on the gist; when the object is vague, keep the original phrase and do not fill it in yourself as arrears, debt, or an amount already paid. Degrees, thresholds, and scope of application within a condition may not be deleted, and reaching a specific degree of damage may not be flattened into any damage. For multiple agreements, keep the condition-to-consequence mapping item by item: A→B and C→D cannot be merged into A or C→B and D; later text only supplements the clauses it explicitly changes, and does not assign new conditions to old consequences. There is no requirement to keep vague transaction wording: when the original meaning is clear, write the concrete object and result, and do not uniformly recast it in general financial terms; when the meaning is ambiguous, keep the key original wording and qualifier, and do not interpret it yourself as a refund, debt forgiveness, or performance.
- Probability and degree may be neither strengthened nor weakened: highly likely must not drop to possible, seems must not become certain, and the original at least/at most/only/still/not yet are kept. A judgment based on physical evidence keeps "inferred from …", and the existence of the evidence must not become proof that the person involved actually did something.
[Key Phrase Fidelity]
- For facts already selected, first copy the key phrases from the material, then compress the surrounding narrative; writing a different set of near-synonymous wording from memory is forbidden. Condition, object, consequence, and period form one set, and source, degree, and negation are kept along with them; it is not a matter of first collecting similar keywords and reassembling a sentence. All fields reuse the same set of evidence, and a qualifier of the same original sentence must not disappear in another field.
- Scope and object are bound together: "later batches are cancelled" does not equal "earlier batches are cancelled". While ambiguity remains, keep only the necessary original wording and qualifier, and do not invent interpretations of refund, arrears, debt forgiveness, or performance; only when the meaning is clear may it be recast as a concrete object and result, and financial terms are not mandatory.
- Directional words must be taken from the material together with the object they modify and the reference point. The display stand in the center and the stairs at the edge cannot swap positions; one object's position is not lent to a neighboring object. Do not supply a location's orientation from urban common sense, directional characters in place names, or the goal of travel. A place description extracts only stable features that are evidenced and does not create a new introductory text; missing references or orientations are not guessed.
- Range endpoints are not assigned to actions: 09:10—09:40 is a range for the whole passage, and an untimed departure must not be written as leaving at 09:40; to mark time, put the range before the whole passage, and only write a separate time when the event itself has another explicit time point. Absolute dates are converted only when there is a reliable anchor.
- The above only demonstrates the binding method and is not plot material. The check record cites only the necessary short evidence and conclusions; it does not copy whole passages or list full tables; the final JSON adds no evidence key and does not write the checking process into the plot.
- Compression may delete non-key propositions but may not rewrite the relations among the remaining ones. The actor, object, condition, medium or path, and result each stay in their own place; a place and the facilities next to it may not be turned into the actor or the cause, and two adjacent sentences do not imply causation.
- If a mechanism is worth keeping, write its necessary path and conditions clearly; when space runs out, delete lower-priority details rather than omitting the key intermediary and forcing a causal link. Quantities, time limits, and negations must still modify their original object.
- Keep the strength of evidence, the degree, and the speaker; a character's statement keeps its source: very few ≠ zero, almost ≠ absolutely, at least ≠ about, reaching a threshold does not equal exceeding it. An inference the original makes from signs is still written as "seems / inferred from this" and may not be omitted into something confirmed; conjecture, possibility, claim, and intention are not upgraded into fact or completion. "Didn't let more than two through" may be compressed to "let very few through", but not written as "let none through at all"; a flashback does not refresh the current state.
- Keep the scope of time application: a same-day spot check or what is seen right now is not upgraded into a long-term pattern, and an old position or a previous plan to stay behind does not equal still being in the same place now. This constraint also applies to scene desc, sceneFocus, and other state fields. Even when compressed to one sentence, the source must be kept per proposition, and "learned/confirmed" may not be used to vouch for an unverified statement or prediction.
- Fidelity holds independently for each field: summary, sceneFocus, place desc, relation, plans.resolve.reason, and other state fields are each checked on their own, and the fact that summary kept a source does not permit another field to omit it. Keep only the small number of propositions that advance the current situation, each carrying its own necessary source and qualifier. "Sustained no new damage / did not get damp" also does not equal having no old marks at all, and a local negation must not be expanded into being fully intact.
- A resolution must state the actual manner: return, cancellation, refusal, failure, and waiver are not the same kind of completion; an obligation already returned or waived must not be left with wording that it is still pending.
